## Project Overview
This Power BI project analyzes customer churn patterns for a fictional bank dataset.  
The primary objective is to identify key factors influencing customer attrition,  
help decision-makers target retention strategies, and reduce churn rates.

---

## Dataset
- *Source*: Publicly available fictional bank dataset (sourced online)
- *Records*: 10,000 customers
- *Key Fields*:  
  - Customer demographics (Age, Gender, Geography)  
  - Account details (Number of Products, Credit Card ownership, Active Membership)  
  - Financial data (Credit Score, Balance, Salary)  
  - Churn status (Yes/No)  

---

##  Objectives
1. Measure overall churn rate and retention rate.
2. Analyze churn trends across demographics and account features.
3. Identify high-risk customer segments for proactive engagement.
4. Enable interactive filtering for deeper insight exploration.

---

##  Dashboard Features
- *KPI Cards*:  
  - Total Customers: 10K  
  - Churn Rate: 20%  
  - Retention Rate: 80%  
  - Average Credit Score: 651  
  - Average Salary: $100K  
  - Average Churn Age: 45 years

- *Geographical Map*:  
  Visual distribution of churned vs. retained customers across countries.

- *Donut Charts*:  
  - Churn by Credit Card Ownership  
  - Churn by Active Membership  
  - Churn by Gender  

- *Bar Chart*:  
  Churn rate segmented by Age Group & Gender.

- *Line Chart*:  
  Average Credit Score trend by Age Group (Churn vs. Retained).

- *Interactive Filters (Slicers)*:  
  - Gender  
  - Number of Products  
  - Geography  
  - Churn status  
  - Tenure range

---

##  Tools & Technologies
- *Power BI Desktop*
- *Data Transformation*: Power Query (M language)
- *Visualization*: Power BI Visuals (Map, Donut, Bar, Line charts)
- *Interactivity*: Slicers, Filters

---

##  Usage
1. Open the .pbix file in Power BI Desktop.
2. Use the slicers to filter by demographics, geography, or account attributes.
3. Hover over charts for detailed tooltips.
4. Explore correlations between variables and churn rate.

---

##  Key Insights
- Churn is highest in customers aged *36-50*, especially among females.
- Non-active members and those with credit cards show higher churn tendencies.
- Geographic variation suggests targeted regional retention campaigns.
- Churn is highest in Germany and lowest in Spain.
- France has the highest number of customers.
- Highest churn rate was recorded with customers who had used all the bank's products with the churn rate increasing with increasing product range.
- Highest churn rate was recorded with customers with 0-1 year tenure range with the churn rate decreasing with increasing tenure range.
